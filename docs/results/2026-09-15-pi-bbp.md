# 2026-09-15: pi digits by BBP on the card and the host, an integer benchmark

`card/examples/pi-bbp.c` generates hexadecimal digits of pi with the
Bailey-Borwein-Plouffe formula: 6-digit blocks handed to N threads,
64-bit modular exponentiation per series term, sums in x87 extended
precision, neighbouring blocks certifying each other (see `pi-bbp.md`).
The source was pushed with `phictl put` and compiled on the card by its
own clang (`cc -O2 -o pi-bbp pi-bbp.c -lpthread`, 1.33 s). The host is the
Ryzen 7 5800X (8 cores, 16 threads, up to 4.85 GHz); the card runs 57
cores at 1.1 GHz, four threads each. Both sum in 80-bit x87 arithmetic.

## Verification

- The first 64 digits match the published constant in every run.
- The first 4,978 digits match an independent computation with
  `echo "obase=16; scale=6000; 4*a(1)" | bc -l` (17 s on the host).
- The card's 80k and 160k digit files, fetched with `phictl get`, are byte
  for byte identical to the host's (`cmp`), as are the Barrett variant's
  outputs on both machines.
- Uncertain blocks: 0 in every run; worst neighbour gap 4.6e-10 at 4,980
  digits, 1.1e-9 at 20k, 2.3e-9 at 80k, 3.4e-9 at 160k (units of the last
  kept digit).

## All threads on both CPUs

| digits | card, 228 threads | host, 16 threads | card / host |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20,000 | 3.104 s (43.0 M terms/s) | 0.491 s (271 M terms/s) | 6.3x slower |
| 80,000 | 65.160 s (32.7 M terms/s) | 9.292 s (230 M terms/s) | 7.0x slower |
| 160,000 | 293.465 s (29.1 M terms/s) | 40.470 s (211 M terms/s) | 7.3x slower |

Terms per second fall with the digit count on both machines because each
term's modular exponentiation grows with log2 of the position.

## Reading

- Whole card against whole host: 0.16 at 20k digits, 0.14 at 80k and
  160k. On the Mandelbrot render (`2026-09-15-mandelbrot.md`) the same
  ratio is 0.25. Integer-heavy code fares worse on the card than x87
  floating point does.
- Per thread the card is 48x slower than a Zen 3 thread here (10.22 s
  against 0.213 s for 4,980 digits) against 27x on Mandelbrot.
- SMT: the four hardware threads of a core add 1.10x over one thread per
  core (57 to 228 threads) with the plain code, 1.20x with Barrett;
  Mandelbrot gets 1.52x.
- The divider hypothesis, tested: the plain modular exponentiation does
  about eighteen `DIV r64` per term, and the single-thread card rate
  (0.8 M terms/s at 1.1 GHz) would fit a microcoded divider at about 76
  cycles if the divisions were the whole cost. Barrett reduction removes
  all but one of them, and the card gains only 1.13x single-threaded and
  1.31x on 228 threads. So the divider is part of the cost, not the bulk:
  the exponentiation is a dependent chain of 64-bit multiplies, compares
  and branches that the in-order core executes at full latency, and the
  per-term x87 work (one conversion, one division, one truncation with a
  control-word switch) is not small either. That SMT recovers more with
  Barrett than with divisions fits a latency-bound chain.
- On the host Barrett is a wash: 19 percent slower single-threaded (Zen 3
  divides 64-bit integers in well under 20 cycles), 6 percent faster on 16
  threads where the two threads of a core share one divider. A first
  attempt with a floating-point reciprocal was slower everywhere and 3.3x
  slower in the card's x87 build on the host, because x87 float-to-integer
  truncation switches the control word; it was dropped.
- The card's binary on the host runs at 0.95 of the native build (0.518
  against 0.491 s): the arithmetic is integer either way, so the x87 ABI
  costs little here, against 0.41 on Mandelbrot.
